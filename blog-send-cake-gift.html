How to Send a Cake to
Someone in the Berkshires

Some of the best orders come from people who live nowhere near here — a daughter in California, a son in Texas, sending something fresh to family back home. It works, and it's simpler than most people expect.

This started happening on its own. Someone hundreds or thousands of miles away would find the bakery online, message about a birthday they were going to miss, and ask whether it was possible to have something delivered to their mom, their grandmother, their old college roommate. It's now one of the most common kinds of order that comes through.

If you're in that position, here's exactly how it works.

Why local beats mail-order

The alternative is a national gifting site that ships a box across the country. That box was baked days ago, packed for durability rather than taste, and spent time in a truck. It's fine. It isn't special.

A local order is baked from scratch the day before it's handed over, and it arrives the way it left the kitchen — nothing frozen, nothing shipped, nothing engineered to survive a week in transit. Your recipient gets a real cake, and often gets it handed to them at their door.

The steps

  • Send the request from wherever you are. The order form doesn't care where you live. Fill in your details, your recipient's town, and the date you want it to land.
  • Kelsey follows up within 24 hours by email or phone to confirm availability, the final price, and the delivery plan. You'll never need to set foot in Massachusetts.
  • Payment is handled remotely before the treats go out — she'll walk you through the options directly when she confirms.
  • It's baked fresh for the date and delivered to your recipient, or held for pickup in Pittsfield if someone local is in on it.

Include these in your first message

Gift orders have a couple of details a normal order doesn't, so put them in up front:

  • Your recipient's name, town, and phone number. The phone number matters — see the note on surprises below.
  • Your gift message, exactly as you'd like it passed along.
  • What they actually like. Chocolate person or fruit person? Loves cake or would rather have a box of cookies? You know them; Kelsey doesn't.
  • Any allergies you're aware of.
  • Whether it's a surprise, and how much the recipient can be told.

On keeping the surprise: a delivery still has to reach a person. The usual approach is to coordinate quietly with someone else in the household, or to call the recipient only when the car is in the driveway. Say in your request that it's a surprise and it'll be handled that way.

What travels well as a gift

  • Chocolate-dipped strawberries — the classic surprise box, and the most-gifted item on the menu.
  • A custom celebration cake for a birthday you're missing.
  • A Build Your Own Brunch Box — bagels, muffins, donuts, cinnamon buns, and a sweet finish. Good for a family that'll be together in the morning.
  • Cookies and brownies for a get-well, a thank-you, or a sorry-I-can't-be-there.
  • Frozen custard pints if your recipient is local enough for the handoff to work.

Timing

Most treats need 24 to 48 hours. A custom cake needs about a week. Time zones don't complicate anything, but do give yourself a day of margin for the back-and-forth to confirm details — especially if you're several hours behind Eastern time and only free to reply in the evenings.

You can also book well ahead. If you know your dad's birthday is in six weeks and you'd rather handle it now while you're thinking about it, the date gets reserved and the cake gets baked fresh for the day.

Send something sweet from anywhere

Tell Kelsey who it's for, where they are, and the occasion. She'll take it from there — you just wait for the happy phone call.
