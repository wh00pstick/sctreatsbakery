How Far in Advance Should
You Order a Custom Cake?

The short version: 24–48 hours for most treats, about a week for a custom cake, and 2–4 weeks for weddings and events. The longer version explains why — and what to do if your party is Saturday.

This is the question that arrives in the inbox more than any other, usually with an apology attached. So here's a clear answer you can plan around, along with the reasoning, because knowing why a cake needs a week makes it much easier to know when you can push.

The timeline, by what you're ordering

24–48 hours: cookies, brownies, cupcakes, and treats

Cupcakes, cookies, brownies, cinnamon rolls, brunch boxes, and frozen custard pints need a day or two. These are baked in small batches close to your pickup so they arrive fresh, and the notice is mostly about slotting your batch into the week's baking and getting ingredients in.

About 1 week: custom celebration cakes

A custom cake isn't one task, it's a sequence with waiting built in. The cake has to bake and cool completely before it can be leveled and filled. Buttercream needs to be made and colored. A chilled, filled cake decorates far better than a warm one. Sculpted or themed work adds another layer of drying and setting time. A week gives room for all of that plus a buffer if something needs a second pass — which, on a cake you only get one shot at, matters.

2–4 weeks: weddings, events, and cake bars

Wedding and event packages, and the Build-Your-Own Cake Bar, need the longest runway. It isn't just baking — it's a date being reserved, a design being finalized, a flavor lineup being chosen, and service being scheduled for a specific window at a specific venue. A 50% deposit holds the date at booking.

A note on busy seasons. Lead times are minimums, not guarantees of availability. Graduation weekends, Mother's Day, the winter holidays, and Saturdays during fall foliage season fill up well beyond these windows. If your date falls in one of those, earlier is genuinely better.

"But my party is this weekend"

Ask anyway. Rush orders are considered case by case, and the answer depends entirely on what's already on the schedule that week. A dozen cupcakes on short notice is a very different request from a three-tier sculpted cake, and often the answer is yes.

If you're short on time, these things make a yes more likely:

  • Be flexible on design. A beautifully frosted cake with a simple finish can happen much faster than a themed sculpt.
  • Be flexible on flavor. Asking "what's easiest this week?" opens doors that a fixed request closes.
  • Consider cupcakes or a treat box instead. They're quicker to turn around and still feel like an occasion.
  • Offer to pick up. Removing a delivery window from a packed day helps more than you'd think.

What to have ready when you reach out

Orders get confirmed fastest when the first message answers the obvious questions. Have these on hand:

  • Your date — and whether it's firm or flexible.
  • Headcount — roughly how many people you're feeding.
  • The occasion and any theme — an inspiration photo is worth a paragraph of description.
  • Flavors, or a note that you'd like suggestions.
  • Allergies or dietary needs, including whether you need gluten-free.
  • Pickup or delivery, and the town if it's being delivered.

That's the whole order form, essentially — which is why it's built to ask exactly those things.

Planning ahead? Book it now

You don't have to wait until the week before. Birthdays, anniversaries, and showers can be scheduled as far ahead as you like — the date gets reserved and the cake gets baked fresh for the day. For anyone who tends to remember a birthday three days out, booking early is the fix.
